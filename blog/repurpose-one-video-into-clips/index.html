Turn One Long Video Into a Month of Clips

By Leon Harris, Founder & AI Creative Director · Updated September 2026

You are staring at a blank composer wondering what to post this week, while a property walkthrough from March and last month's Facebook Live sit unwatched on your Page. Picture opening that folder and pulling ten postable clips out of footage you already paid to make. This guide shows you how: which videos are actually worth cutting up, how to find the good 30 seconds without scrubbing for an hour, and what the free tools really give you in 2026.

Not every video is worth cutting up

Before you open any editor, run your footage through two filters. First: is the audio clear enough to transcribe? A video shot with wind noise, a noisy crowd, or a mic buried in a bag will not give you a usable transcript, and without a transcript you are stuck scrubbing blindly. Second: is there at least one moment where something is actually said or shown — a price, a step, an objection answered, a mistake named, a before-and-after? A silent 20-minute pan across an empty condo unit has nothing to cut. Length does not matter here. A 6-minute video with one sharp answer beats a 60-minute one that is all small talk and introductions.

Get the transcript before you touch a video editor

This is the step most owners skip, and it is the one that saves the most time. Do not scrub a 45-minute recording looking for clips — read it instead. Upload your video as Unlisted on YouTube and download the automatic transcript; it is free, and YouTube lists Filipino as a supported automatic-caption language. Google's own help documentation is upfront that accuracy drops with accents, background noise, and multiple languages spoken together — which matters when you are about to see how it handles Taglish. If you would rather read and cut in the same place, Descript's free plan gives you 60 media minutes a month, 100 one-time AI credits, and a text editor where you cut video by deleting the words you do not want. Note that free Descript exports are watermarked and capped at 720p, so treat it as a place to find and rough-cut your moments, then export the finished version elsewhere. Either way, you now have a searchable document instead of a timeline.

Read for the moment, not the highlight reel

Skim the transcript for anything concrete: a number, a named objection with its answer, a "here's the mistake people make," a specific step. Mark the ones roughly 20 to 45 seconds long. Then apply the real test — can this play on its own, with zero context from the rest of the video? If it depends on something you said 10 minutes earlier, or points at a slide the viewer never saw, it is not a clip yet. Cut it anyway and it will confuse people, not convert them. Jump to the timestamp in the actual video to confirm it plays cleanly — no mid-sentence start, no reaction cut off — before you commit to it.

Cutting, reframing, and captions

For the mechanics, two tools checked as of September 2026. CapCut's free plan covers timeline editing, auto-captions, and 1080p export with no watermark on a plain manual export — watermarks appear only on Pro-labelled templates and effects. Opus Clip's free tier gives around 60 processing minutes a month, 1080p, 9:16 only, a visible watermark, and files that expire after 3 days. Use it to see which moments an AI would pick, not as your finished setup. None of these free tiers are built for a real monthly habit; they are for testing whether the whole exercise is worth paying for.

If more than one person is on camera, do not trust auto-reframe blind. Vertical reframing works by tracking a face or the loudest voice, and it is reliable with one speaker — the moment a second person enters frame, it can crop out whoever is talking or lock onto the wrong face. Scrub every reframed clip before exporting. And turn captions on for everything: feed video on Facebook and TikTok often starts muted, so a clip without captions loses viewers in the first two seconds no matter how good the moment is.

Where this goes wrong

Auto-captions on Filipino or Taglish audio need correction every time. YouTube supports Filipino, but Google's own documentation admits accuracy drops with accents and with multiple languages spoken together — and Taglish, where you switch between Tagalog and English mid-sentence, is exactly that case. Brand names and Metro Manila street or barangay names get mangled most often. Never publish auto-captions without reading them first.

AI clip-pickers are also not safe to trust unreviewed. They rank clips by generic engagement signals, not by whether the moment is still true or appropriate to post. The tool has no idea that the price you quoted six months ago has changed, that a client visible in the shot never agreed to be posted, or that a claim you made is one you can no longer back. Watch every candidate clip end to end before it goes anywhere.

Consent is a real legal question here, not just courtesy. The National Privacy Commission's Circular No. 2025-01, effective 10 June 2025, treats recording and uploading footage of identifiable people as personal-data processing — you are expected to be transparent that footage may be posted online, and to blur or mask minors and vulnerable individuals such as the elderly or PWDs who appear incidentally. NPC guidance also holds that people do not lose privacy rights simply by being in a public place. That applies directly to a walkthrough with a client visible, a webinar with attendees on camera, or event footage with bystanders in frame. If someone identifiable is central to the clip, get their okay before posting.

Last thing: a month of clips from one video is not a content plan by itself. If every clip opens with the long video's title and closes with the same call to action, it reads as one post repeated 20 times. Give each clip its own first line, pulled from what is actually said in that moment, and vary what you ask the viewer to do.

Frequently asked questions

Is my old Facebook Live or webinar recording actually worth turning into clips?

Only if it passes two tests: the audio is clear enough to transcribe (no wind, crowd noise, or muffled mic drowning the speaker), and there is at least one moment where something concrete is said or shown — a number, a specific step, an objection answered, a before-and-after. A silent walkthrough of an empty property, or 40 minutes of small talk, will not yield usable clips no matter how much footage you have.

Do I need to watch the whole long video to find clips?

No. Get a transcript first — YouTube's free automatic captions on an Unlisted upload, or Descript's free 60 minutes a month — and read the text to find candidate moments, then jump to those timestamps to check the video plays cleanly there. Scrubbing a 45-minute recording by eye is the slow way to do this.

Which free tool should a small business start with — CapCut, Opus Clip, or Descript?

If one person is talking to camera and you are comfortable picking the moments yourself from a transcript, CapCut's free plan covers cutting and captioning end to end at 1080p with no watermark on a plain manual export. Opus Clip's free tier is useful for seeing which moments an AI would pick, but the roughly 60 minutes a month, 3-day file expiry, and watermark make it a trial rather than a working setup. Descript is worth it if you want to read the transcript and cut in the same place, though its free exports are watermarked and capped at 720p.

Will auto-captions work for Taglish or Filipino audio?

YouTube supports Filipino for automatic captions, but Google's own documentation flags accuracy dropping with accents, background noise, and multiple languages spoken together — which is exactly how most Philippine business owners talk on camera. Expect to correct brand names, street and barangay names, and Taglish phrasing every time. Do not publish auto-captions unread.

Is it legal to post clips of a webinar, event, or property walkthrough with other people in it?

Under the National Privacy Commission's Circular No. 2025-01, effective 10 June 2025, recording and uploading footage of identifiable people counts as personal-data processing. You are expected to be transparent that footage will be posted online, and to blur or mask minors and vulnerable individuals such as the elderly or PWDs who appear incidentally. NPC guidance also holds that people do not lose privacy rights simply by being in a public place. If a client, agent, or attendee is clearly identifiable and central to the clip, get their consent before posting.

Can I just post whatever clips an AI tool ranks highest?

No. AI clip-pickers rank moments by generic engagement patterns, not by whether the content is still accurate or appropriate for your business. The tool cannot tell that a price you quoted is outdated, that a client in frame did not consent to being posted, or that a claim you made is one you can no longer back. Watch every candidate clip end to end before it goes out.
