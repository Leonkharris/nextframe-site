Build a 30-Day Social Media Content Calendar with AI (and Actually Stick to It)

By Leon Harris, Founder & AI Creative Director · Updated August 2026

You post when you remember, go quiet for two weeks, then panic-post a promo when sales dip. What you actually want is a page that looks alive even in your busiest week — without sitting down every morning trying to think of something. This guide gives you the system: a posting schedule you can keep, a short list of repeatable post types, and a one-sitting method that turns what is already in your head into 30 dated posts.

Pick a posting schedule you can actually keep

Be honest about your free hours. If you are running the business and the page, do not plan for 14 posts a week — plan for 3. There is no verified "best" posting frequency for Philippine small businesses, so work from arithmetic instead: 3 posts a week for four straight weeks is 12 real posts. A burst of daily posting that dies after 10 days gets you fewer than that and breaks the habit. Consistency beats volume because volume without consistency is just a spike. Pick Monday, Wednesday, Friday, or whatever fits your week, and commit to that exact number for the full 30 days before you think about adding more.

Build 4 to 6 buckets so you are not always selling

A bucket is just a repeatable type of post. For a local business here, six work well: offer or promo (what is on sale, what is new), proof (reviews, before-and-after, repeat customers), behind the scenes (making the product, the shop, your team), education (how to use, choose, or care for what you sell), a real question (something a customer actually asked you in Messenger or in person), and community or local (a nearby event, a fiesta, a shout-out to another local business). Write down which apply to you and how often each should appear. The panic-promo pattern happens because "offer" is the only bucket you have — spread it out so it is maybe 1 post in 5.

One brain-dump, turned into 30 dated ideas

This replaces staring at a blank caption box. Set aside 15 to 20 minutes and voice-note or type everything true about your business right now: current best-sellers, promos coming this month, three to five questions customers keep asking, any local event or holiday in the next 30 days, a recent win, one thing your regulars always say. Then open a free AI chat tool — ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude all have no-cost tiers — paste the brain-dump plus your bucket list and chosen schedule, and ask for a table with four columns — Date, Bucket, One-line idea, Platform — covering all 30 days, buckets rotating so nothing repeats back-to-back.

Be honest about what this step cannot do. The tool does not know your stock level today, does not know there is a barangay fiesta next weekend unless you tell it, and does not know your regulars by name. If your brain-dump is "I sell food, give me ideas," you get ideas that read exactly like that. The brain-dump is the actual work; the AI only formats it into a calendar.

Batch your captions in one sitting

Right after the 30-idea list, write every caption in one sitting — not one a day, in a different mood each time. At 3 posts a week that is 12 or 13 captions back-to-back, written by you or drafted by the AI and edited into your own voice. This is the step that prevents the go-quiet-for-two-weeks failure: the captions already exist before the busy week that would have derailed you.

Adapt one idea, do not copy-paste it

The same idea should read differently per platform. On Facebook, write the fuller version in a local-group tone, paired with a photo. On TikTok, the caption barely matters — what matters is the hook in the first second of the video itself, casual voice, maybe a trending sound. On Instagram, keep it short, add hashtags, write it for a carousel or Reel. Three variants of one idea, not one caption stretched across three apps.

Where the calendar actually lives

Keep the full 30-day plan in a free spreadsheet — Google Sheets with the columns Date, Platform, Bucket, Caption, Status is enough. That spreadsheet is your master list, because the built-in schedulers will not hold a clean 30 days for you. Meta Business Suite's Planner is free for Facebook Pages and Instagram business or creator accounts, but how far ahead it schedules is genuinely unclear right now: Meta's help documentation has been cited at 75 days, while many Page owners in 2026 report closer to 30. Open Planner and check the furthest date your own account accepts. TikTok Studio's built-in scheduler, free for creator or business accounts on desktop, is stricter: per TikTok's own business blog it schedules 15 minutes to 10 days ahead, and a scheduled post cannot be edited afterwards, only deleted and re-uploaded. So the spreadsheet holds all 30 days, you re-queue TikTok every 10 days from it, and you load Facebook and Instagram as far out as Planner allows.

Review monthly, using your own numbers

At month-end, open Meta Business Suite Insights and the Analytics tab in TikTok Studio — both free and already built in — and see which bucket and which platform version actually got saves, shares, comments, or watch time. Skip the generic "best time to post" numbers; use what your own dashboard shows. Do more of what worked, and feed that into next month's brain-dump.

Frequently asked questions

How many times a week should a small business in the Philippines post on social media?

There is no verified universal 'best' number, so be wary of any article that states one as fact without a named source. The practical rule for a one-person team: pick a schedule you can hold for a full month without missing a post, even if that is only 3 times a week. Three posts a week, every week, beats a burst of 14 that stops after ten days, because consistency is what a content calendar is actually solving for.

Can I schedule a whole month of Facebook and Instagram posts for free?

Maybe, and you should check rather than assume. Meta Business Suite's Planner is free for Facebook Pages and Instagram business or creator accounts, but the advance limit is contested right now: Meta's own help documentation has been cited at 75 days, while many Page owners in 2026 report a window closer to 30 days. Open Planner, try to set a date six weeks out, and see what your own account accepts before you build a calendar around a number you read somewhere.

Can I schedule a full 30 days of TikTok posts natively for free?

No. TikTok's own scheduler, in TikTok Studio on desktop, lets creator or business accounts schedule from 15 minutes to 10 days ahead, per TikTok's own business blog announcing the feature, and a scheduled post cannot be edited afterwards, only deleted and re-uploaded. Keep the full 30-day plan in a spreadsheet and re-queue TikTok in roughly 10-day batches, or use a paid third-party scheduler if you need it queued further out.

Will my AI-generated captions sound generic to my regular customers?

They will, if the input was generic. The tool cannot know your stock this week, a local event only your barangay knows about, or the names of your regulars. It only knows what you tell it. The brain-dump is the actual work: feed it your real best-sellers, the specific questions customers ask you in person, and what is happening locally this month, and the output stops reading like a template.

What if something changes mid-month, like running out of stock or a cancelled event?

A calendar is a plan, not a contract. Swap or delete the affected post in your spreadsheet and in the scheduler. Meta Business Suite lets you edit or delete a scheduled post before it goes live; TikTok requires deleting and re-uploading, since scheduled TikToks cannot be edited. Keep the rest of the month's buckets rotating as planned.

How do I know if the calendar is working?

Check monthly, using only the free insights each platform already gives you: Meta Business Suite Insights for Facebook and Instagram, and the Analytics tab in TikTok Studio. Look at which bucket (offer, proof, behind the scenes, education, real question, community) and which platform version got real engagement on your own page, then adjust next month's bucket mix. Do not rely on generic 'best time to post' numbers you have not checked against your own data.
