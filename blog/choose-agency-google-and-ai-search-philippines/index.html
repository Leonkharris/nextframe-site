How to Choose an Agency for Google and AI Search in the Philippines

By Leon Harris, Founder & AI Creative Director · Updated November 2026

You want customers to find your business on Google and also when they ask ChatGPT or Gemini, and every agency pitch now promises both. Most of those promises cannot be kept. This article gives you the questions, red flags and a short paid pilot that let you tell an honest provider from a confident one before you sign anything.

One disclosure first: this site belongs to an AI marketing agency, so every question and pilot test below applies to us as much as to anyone you shortlist.

What stays the same when you buy for both

Google's own page, AI features and your website, says a page can appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode if Google has already listed it and it is eligible to show in normal Search. Google states there are no additional requirements and no special optimisation for these features, and that you do not need new AI files or special code added to your pages. In plain terms, you are mostly buying solid search basics: a website Google can read, pages that link to each other sensibly, useful content that answers what customers ask, and business details that match what the page actually says.

So a secret "AI layer" is something Google says you do not need. Background: our explainer on AI search. General checks before paying any provider: this buyer checklist.

What an agency can control, and what it cannot

ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity are separate tools, and Google's page does not cover how they choose sources. Their answers can change between attempts, wording, users and locations, so nobody can promise you a recommendation. Be wary of anyone who does.

An honest agency controls the things on your side of the fence: whether Google can read your site, how clear and useful your pages are, whether your address, hours and phone number are accurate everywhere they appear online, your Google Business Profile, reviews you earn genuinely, and mentions on credible local sites and directories. It does not control which sources an AI tool picks on a given day, or whether a particular answer names you.

Questions to ask on the sales call

Send the same written questions to three providers so you can compare answers.

  • How do you measure AI visibility? Good: a fixed list of questions in English, Filipino and Taglish, asked on a schedule and logged with date, tool and whether you were mentioned. Bad: one screenshot, or a "proprietary AI score" with no method.
  • What do you report monthly? Good: Search Console impressions, clicks, search terms and pages, plus Business Profile calls, direction requests and website clicks, leads, and the question log. Google says visits from its AI features are counted inside the normal Web search totals in Search Console, not as a separate line. Ask what any "AI traffic" figure is really based on.
  • Will you work on my Google Business Profile? Good: they work inside your verified profile and you stay owner. Bad: they set it up under their own account.
  • How do you handle Filipino and Taglish content? Good: native or reviewed writing using the phrases customers really type. Bad: raw machine translation.
  • Who owns the accounts, domain and content? You, with admin access and a handover on exit.
  • Do you use AI to draft, and who checks it? Good: a named person fact-checks prices, addresses and claims before publishing, which matches Google's guidance on AI-generated content. Bad: bulk-publishing dozens of AI pages.

Red flags

Walk away from guaranteed number-one rankings or guaranteed AI mentions, and from methods they refuse to explain. Walk away from review packages: Google's Maps content policy bans paid reviews, reviews traded for discounts or freebies, and reviews not based on a real experience. Link packages are another flag, since Google's spam policies cover buying links and mass-producing low-value pages, including with AI tools.

A paid pilot instead of a long contract

Ask each finalist to quote a 60 to 90 day pilot with these deliverables. For what a pilot may cost, see our cost guide.

  • Weeks 1 to 2: a starting audit covering whether Google lists your pages, Search Console set up in your name, a Business Profile review, and a baseline of AI answers in English, Filipino and Taglish.
  • Day 30: technical fixes done, Business Profile cleaned up, two to four service or answer pages published after human fact-checking.
  • Day 60: more pages, a genuine review-request process, and third-party mentions you can see.
  • Day 90: a report with Search Console before and after, Business Profile actions, the repeated question log, honest notes on what did and did not move, and a go or no-go call.

Define success as agreed leads and a Search Console trend, never a guaranteed AI mention.

When you do not need an agency

If Google already lists your site, your Google Business Profile is verified and accurate, you have a handful of clear service pages, and someone can spend a few hours a month in Google Search Console (Google's free tool that shows which searches bring people to your site), do it yourself. Start with the free manual check of whether ChatGPT recommends your business and save dated screenshots. Hire an agency only for time, technical fixes or content volume you cannot cover.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a special agency for AI search, or is SEO enough?

Google states there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode and no special optimisation is needed beyond being listed and eligible to show in Search. So strong search basics come first. An AI-aware agency can add regular checks of what AI tools say about you, consistent business details across the web, and Filipino and Taglish content, but it cannot guarantee any assistant will name you.

Can any agency guarantee my business will appear in ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews?

No. Answers change between attempts, users and tools, and each assistant chooses its own sources. An agency can improve your chances by making your site easy for Google to read, improving your content and keeping your business details consistent, but a guarantee of a number-one spot or of AI mentions is a red flag.

An agency says I need llms.txt or special code for AI answers. Is that true?

For Google's AI features, Google says you do not need new machine-readable files, AI text files or extra markup, and no special schema.org structured data is required. Other AI tools publish their own guidance, so ask the agency to show you the source for any claim like this.

How can I see whether Google's AI features send me traffic?

Google says visits from its AI features are included in your overall search traffic in Search Console, under the Web search type in the Performance report. They are not shown as a separate AI line, so ask any agency exactly what their AI traffic number is based on.

Should my agency touch my Google Business Profile?

Yes if you are a local or service business, but the profile should stay owned by you and verified in your name. Reviews must be genuine: Google's Maps content policy bans paid reviews, reviews traded for discounts or freebies, and reviews not based on a real experience.

Is it okay if an agency uses AI to write my content?

Google's guidance allows AI to help with research and drafting, but says the output can be inaccurate and needs checking, and its spam policies cover mass-producing low-value pages. A good agency uses AI for drafts and has a named person check facts, prices, addresses and claims before anything goes live.
