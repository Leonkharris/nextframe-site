The disagreement

Pre-roll and popunder, versus a front-page banner

The network's proposal was relayed at the top of the call. The client's position came back immediately.

Both sides have a point, and the tension is real. The client's instinct — that a forced five-second wait annoys people on this particular site — is reasonable. But pre-roll is the format with the most inventory and the strongest engagement, which is exactly why the network leads with it.

Three days later the account manager made the same case again: "it's a very big format, a long-form video style format, so it is engaging… for sure you guys should test." The refusal is a client preference, not a finding — worth revisiting with numbers rather than treating as settled.

The workaround

Popunder counts as a banner

With pre-roll off the table, the group reached for the second recommendation and reframed it.

The reframe
"I think the popunder is equal to banner"

Agreed on the call: "Yes, because it has different placements." So the plan became banners first, popunders second — which satisfies the client's stated preference while keeping one of the two recommended formats.

The device
Mobile only

"I think we need to be choosing for the mobile ones." Settled early and never revisited. It matches what the account manager recommended three days later, and it means the desktop sizes in the media kit can be ignored.

📄

The media kit's own framing helped the argument. It describes popunders as "browser-integrated placements that offer a non-intrusive alternative to banner ads" — which is how a format the client would otherwise reject got accepted. Note that the page carries no size or file specification at all, unlike every banner format.

A misunderstanding worth fixing

CPM was explained wrongly in the room

It passed without correction, and the pricing model is the basis of every budget decision that follows.

❌

What was said: "It's a CPM, auto clicks — so every thousand clicks it's one charge."

CPM is cost per thousand impressions, not clicks. You pay when the ad is shown, whether or not anyone touches it. On an adult network with large volume and low click-through, that distinction is the whole economics of the buy — it decides whether a creative that nobody clicks is free or expensive.

The same misunderstanding is visible in the hesitancy that followed: "those CPM ads, it's not as efficient, you know — but the thing is, if our content can catch players' eyes, then maybe it's worth a shot." That instinct is right, for the opposite reason to the one given: because you pay for the impression regardless, the creative has to earn the click or the money is simply gone.
