What happened to the recording

The transcript is complete. The audio is not.

Worth stating precisely, because "the transcript stops early" and "the meeting stopped early" are different problems.

🔇

Measured minute by minute: speech runs normally to 14:59 (mean −33 to −39 dB, peaks around −13 dB). From 15:00 onward, every single minute to the end of the file reads −91.0 dB mean and −91.0 dB peak. That is not a quiet room; it is the numerical floor of the format. The channel is carrying nothing at all.

The video is unaffected and keeps changing throughout. So somebody kept presenting for another forty-nine minutes and none of it was captured.

What this costs
The decisions are unrecoverable

The campaign was configured on screen during this stretch. What was agreed about it — budgets, who does what, when it launches — was spoken, and is gone.

Worth fixing
Check the recorder setup

If the capture tool lost the audio device mid-session, it will do it again. Of the four recordings processed in this batch, this is the only one affected — the other three have clean audio throughout.

The finding in the silence

The network's own data argues against targeting the US

An infographic was shared to the team chat during this stretch and sat on screen. Nobody can have discussed it, because nothing was being recorded — and it undercuts the targeting decision.

Where the audience skews female

Latin America — and the Philippines

  • Philippines 64% female
  • Colombia 56%
  • Argentina 56%
  • Mexico 48%
  • Chile 46% · Peru 44%

Where the campaign is pointed

United States and the anglophone markets

  • United States 28% female
  • Worldwide average 38%
  • Canada 28% · UK 25% · Germany 26%
🌎

Why this matters more than it looks. This operator's existing book is Latin American — the nine-month analytics reviewed five days earlier are denominated in Mexican pesos, with the player base, the promotions and the Spanish-language creative all pointed at that market. The 8 October meeting then spent its time worrying that the casino's look was wrong for American players.

Meanwhile the network's own figures show the LatAm markets this client already serves have audiences that are 44–56% female, against 28% in the US — a completely different composition, in markets where the brand, the language and the payment rails already work.

Nobody has compared the cost of US inventory against LatAm inventory, or asked whether the first test flight should run where the client already converts. That question is not in any of the four recordings.
