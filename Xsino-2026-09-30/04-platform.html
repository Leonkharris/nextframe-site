Integration gap 1

"Game type" does not mean the same thing to both sides

A provider raised it, and the answer is more interesting than the question.

1

The categories we publish are examples, not a schema

Our preset list — slots, live casino, sport, keno, fishing — does not match how the provider classifies its own games, so they asked whether they can define their own.

The answer given: "This game type is just an example… it's not a hard requirement that they must pass back these exact strings." So they can, and nobody told them.

The problem is not the taxonomy. It is that a list published as an example reads as a specification, and an integrating partner has no way to tell the difference.
2

The fix is documentation, and it was accepted

"I can write it more clearly." The follow-up was to go back and confirm with the provider directly, because it was framed as a technical integration question rather than a commercial one.

The person needed was not in the relevant chat group and was added during the call.

Integration gap 2

There is no way to upload a game icon

The sharper of the two findings, raised after looking at the platform's own back office.

🖼️

"You actually lack a function at the back, which is to upload icons… the icons you have now are the pre-supplied ones, so there's no way to replace the images."

Game artwork arrives from each provider in whatever shape they ship it. The example given: one provider's artwork is horizontal, another's is vertical, and both are in English. The lobby has to present them side by side with no ability to normalise either.

Today
Whatever the provider sent

Preset images only. No replacement, no crop, no re-version.

The shape problem
Horizontal vs vertical

Different providers ship different aspect ratios for the same shelf.

The language problem
Artwork is in English

A multi-language lobby needs per-language artwork, which means one icon per language per game.

Why now
Thailand

Named as the reason it stops being cosmetic: "considering that you may be able to enter the Thai market, you may have to add an extra feature."

🛠️

Two options were put forward, and they are not equivalent. Either providers send the full image set down and the back office picks a preview from them, or the back office gains its own upload-and-edit control. The second is more work and the only one that survives a language the provider does not supply.

It was accepted on the call — "OK, I'll adjust it here" — with no scope or date. Note the second-order cost nobody raised: per-language artwork multiplies the asset count by the number of languages, which lands on the design team, not on engineering.
