Part I I

An Evening at Jade

✦

A green velvet corridor, a lit slab of onyx, and a room that does not tell you what time it is.

Jade Supper Club · Media Kit 2026 · 03/22
Jade Supper Club · ManilaAn Evening at Jade · 04/22

the storyJade Supper Club

Manila has always known how to stay up late. What it did not have was one room that could hold the whole night — dinner worth dressing for, a band worth staying for, and a bar that is still pouring when the kitchen has gone quiet. Jade was built to be that room.

It begins as a corridor. Green velvet on both sides, brass underfoot, and at the end a single slab of backlit jade onyx that glows like a held breath. You do not see the dining room until you are inside it — and then it opens: a double-height space in deep emerald and warm brass, banquettes running the long wall, a proscenium stage at the far end, and a spiral staircase in polished brass climbing to a mezzanine that looks down on all of it.

The cooking is fire-led and Manila-anchored: a wood oven at the centre of the kitchen, an ice bar for the raw list, Philippine seafood and Japanese precision meeting Mediterranean technique. The food is developed in partnership with chef Todd English, whose five James Beard Awards and thirty-five years of wood-fired cooking sit behind the pass — his first fine-dining room in the Philippines.

The name is not borrowed from the stone alone. In the Philippines the jade vine — tayabak — is a native flower found nowhere else on earth: cascading blooms of an impossible blue-green, rare and half-hidden, the kind of thing you do not simply see but come upon. Jade takes its name and its colour from that flower — a room with a Filipino soul, not an imported one.

“A supper club is not a restaurant with a band. It is one evening, written in three acts, and the food is only the first.”

An Evening at Jade

  1. 6:00

    Doors

    The corridor lights come up. First seating in the Grand Room; the ice bar is dressed and the oven is at temperature.

  2. 6:30

    The Ice Bar

    Oysters, crudo and hand rolls off the raw list. A pianist plays the room quietly — no vocal yet.

  3. 8:00

    Peak Service

    The wood oven runs flat out. Whole lapu-lapu, dry-aged ribeye and the flatbread that started it all.

  4. 9:00

    First Set

    The proscenium curtain opens. Forty-five minutes — jazz-leaning, with a vocalist, mixed live for the whole floor.

  5. 10:00

    Second Seating

    The room turns. Later tables arrive dressed for the music rather than the menu; the bar takes over the front.

  6. 11:00

    Second Set

    Louder, later, closer to the floor. The mezzanine fills; the spiral staircase becomes the best seat in the house.

  7. 12:00

    The Kitchen Closes

    Last order from the full menu. Bar bites — the Jade Dragon, Midnight in Manila — run until the end.

  8. 1:00

    The Late Room

    Lights down two stops. Resident DJ, sake carafes, and the part of the night the regulars come for.

  9. 3:00

    Last Call

    The brass panels dim one at a time. The corridor is the last thing you see, exactly as it was the first.

Three Acts

Dinner, music, late. Every element of the house — lighting, menu, staffing, sound — is built to hand the night from one act to the next without a seam.

Booking the Night

Two seatings, 6PM and 10PM. The first is booked for dinner, the second for the music — the same room, priced and paced differently. Walk-ins are taken at the bar until the kitchen closes.

Todd English

The Chef Partner — five James Beard Awards, fifteen cookbooks, and his first fine-dining room in the Philippines.

Todd English trained under Jean-Jacques Rachou at La Côte Basque, then cooked through Italy and came home with rustic Mediterranean food over live fire. He opened Olives in Charlestown in 1989 and built it into a group of restaurants across four continents — five James Beard Awards, fifteen cookbooks and years of national television behind him.

At Jade he is not a lent name but the house’s anchor: he shapes the food, returns for every seasonal change and cooks the opening service himself. In a city where fine dining trades on imported chefs, his is the single biggest draw the room carries — the reason Jade opens with a national profile instead of building one over years.

The Bar Partner

The newest addition to the house — and one of the most decorated bartenders in Europe — shaping the Jade bar.

Roman Mutzner is the newest name on the Jade team and the man behind the bar. A Swiss bartender from St. Gallen, he is one of the most decorated competitors of his generation — 2026 Switzerland Champion at Diageo World Class, the industry’s most demanding global contest, and a serial national titleholder across spirits, liqueurs and gin.

He founded R. Mutzner Barfactory in 2022 and first came to Manila on an MBA exchange in 2024. At Jade he designs the bar from the ice programme up — a cocktail list built to stand level with the kitchen, and the reason the room pours until 2AM.

The Road to October

  1. Now

    On Site

    Three months of advance rent and three months of security deposit — ₱3,373,966 — are placed with the landlord. Design is set with BA8 Concepts, and Jerikson Construction is on site: the first four build phases — structure and mezzanine, finishes, plumbing and electrical rough-in — are all under way.

  2. Aug

    Permits & Long Leads

    Permits and licences at ₱400,000 are lodged, and the point-of-sale goes in for BIR accreditation — a fixed three-to-four-week clock that gates opening day more tightly than the build does. Fire suppression, smoke detection and air conditioning are coordinated with building management.

  3. Sep

    Systems & the Kitchen

    Automated lighting, exhaust and ducting, CCTV and biometrics. The cold line, the hot line and the Josper charcoal oven are delivered and wired. Custom furniture installation begins, and the bar's ice cap goes in.

  4. Oct

    Staff, Stock, Rehearsal

    Kitchenware, bar and glassware at ₱1,100,000. Hiring closes on the forty-four-strong opening roster and one month of paid training begins at ₱1,135,960. Menu R&D runs against ₱6,000,000 of opening stock. Plants and soft finishes land last.

  5. Open

    Doors

    Friends and family from 30 October, then press, then full service — Tuesday to Sunday, two seatings at 6PM and 10PM, bar until 3AM.

  6. Yr 1

    The First Twelve Months

    Year One is modelled at ₱76.9M in the base case and ₱130.7M in the high case, against a 72% gross margin.

Already Committed

₱3,373,966 is with the landlord, design and construction are contracted, and the mezzanine is engineered. The remaining spend is scheduled against the weeks above rather than drawn in a lump.

The Long Poles

Three items set the date, not the budget: BIR accreditation of the POS, landlord sign-off on fire suppression and air conditioning, and the month of paid training that must finish before doors.
