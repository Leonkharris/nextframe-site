The vocabulary first

Two words that are not the same

Half the confusion in the room came from two terms being used interchangeably. Ming-da stopped and defined them, and the rest of the discussion depends on the distinction.

回滾 · Rollback

The round is voided

  • The transaction is cancelled
  • The stake is returned to the player
  • State goes back to the moment before the bet
  • No win is ever calculated

結算 · Settlement

The round is finished properly

  • The provider calculates the win
  • The wallet signal is sent to us
  • We credit the player
  • A stuck round is one where this did not complete
💬

Ming-da: "Rollback means that transaction is void, the money rolls back. Settlement means it has not settled — it has not produced a WIN. It should produce the WIN and then send the wallet signal over."

The two failure modes

A stuck round is stuck for one of two reasons

Type 1
Settled, but never told us

The provider closed the round and calculated a payout, but never sent the wallet signal. Their books show a win; ours show nothing. This is the dangerous one: the player won and was not paid.

Type 2
Never calculated at all

The provider has not computed the round. No signal exists because no result exists. Nothing to deliver yet.

⚠️

Mao asked whether a missing signal simply means the round is absent from the provider's list. Ming-da's answer: it is not that simple any more. "In our understanding, if it was sent it was settled; if it wasn't sent it wasn't settled. But now, no wallet signal splits into two cases."

The monitor

懸單監控 — find them before the operator does

1

The watch list

Two counters sit at the top: 323 rounds already past their timeout and 0 currently under observation. The list is filtered to overdue items and can auto-refresh.

Timeout thresholds differ by game type, because the games differ. Sports gets 7 days, which Ming-da admitted is generous and asked the team to flag if it is too loose. Table and live games get 5 minutes to observe and 15 to overdue. Other electronic games get 3 minutes and 15.

Each row carries the round number, provider, tenant, game, type, player, currency, stake, first bet time, round age and how long it has been overdue. The ages on screen run from about 11 to 15 hours.

These rows are hours old, not days — the oldest on screen is 14h35m. The seven days mentioned in this discussion is the sports timeout threshold, not the age of these rounds; slots (其他電子) run a 3-minute observation and 15-minute timeout. Rounds older than a week do exist, but they sit further down the list and belong to other providers — see below.
2

Looking one round up

Clicking a row opens 局號查詢, which calls the provider's API live and puts the two ledgers side by side. Our record on the left, theirs on the right.

This example is a Type 2. The banner reads 供應商未結，待派 win — provider has not settled, a win is pending. Our side shows the round unsettled with a 5.00 TWD bet accepted at SC_OK. Their side shows the round not closed out, with a gameEnd payout of 3.00 already computed. Round age: zero days.

The buttons above handle the exceptions: copy the bet transaction ID, export to GameArt, export to RP, pay out manually, or mark the round void, expired or confirmed.
3

The expensive case, caught

This is a Type 1, and it shows exactly why the monitor exists. The banner reads 供應商已結算，我方未入帳 — the provider settled, we never credited.

Our ledger: a bet of 2,187.50 TWD accepted cleanly. Their ledger: round closed, payout 51.00, three days old. The win transaction did come through — and was rejected with SC_TRANSACTION_DUPLICATED, the raw provider response visible in the row.

Without this screen, the player simply never got paid and nobody knew.

The unresolved question

Molly's question: who pays the missing win?

Molly Wu pressed the point that matters commercially, and it did not get a complete answer.

Q

The question

RPGaming's rule is that if a round has not come back after seven days, they settle it as a player loss and deduct accordingly. Molly's objection: the amount shown in a stuck round is the player's stake, but the money that never arrived is the money they won.

So if the player actually won, and GameArt dropped the round, who absorbs it? And, she added, a new customer is about to connect to the same provider — they may not allow seven days at all, and may settle on their own weekly cycle.

A

The answer, as far as it goes

It has to be taken up with GameArt directly, supported by a full comparison against the provider's own ledger. The player's screenshot is the starting evidence, because it carries the round ID, and the round ID is what makes a query possible.

Ming-da was clear about the limit: the RGS is not hosted by Lucifer, so the game's internal multipliers are not visible from their side. "That thing is not built on our side, we definitely cannot look at it." What they can do is help the operator handle the complaint immediately and query the provider on their behalf.

A precedent was cited: a player's screenshot showed a win of more than 170 times on a game, but the wallet was never credited and no record could be found. That case is why the comparison process exists.
❗

Still unanswered. No rule was agreed for who bears the cost when the provider loses a winning round. The team's position is that it must be pushed back to GameArt, and that the provider should be required to reply within a fixed window or pay the win. Mao noted they have asked for this before.

The judgement
Age is the problem, not value

The amounts are small. A ten-day-old unsettled round is a reconciliation problem and a support problem long before it is a money problem.

🔎

The 24-hour rule does not reach them. It was framed around GameArt, which now settles automatically after 24 hours. These providers have no such window, no agreed reply time, and in several cases a different currency and tenant. Extending the rule to them is not a configuration change — it needs a conversation with each integration.

One number worth remembering

The deadline has moved twice

GameArt's automatic calculation window started at 32 days, became one week, and is now 24 hours. After 24 hours without settlement, it comes back automatically. That shrinking window is what makes a monitor viable at all.

🚀

Safe to deploy. Raised and answered in the same breath: the wallet core has a graceful shutdown, so deploying does not interrupt the wallet loop. Deployments do not need to wait for a quiet period.
