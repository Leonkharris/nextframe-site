Why Manila nightlife needed a magazine

Manila nights are sold on flyers and in DMs. Party Life is here to cover the rooms, host its own nights, and let readers choose the next cover.

How a night gets sold here

Most nights in Manila reach you the same way. A flyer with a DJ lineup lands in your feed, a friend forwards it, and the caption says to DM for tables. The tickets sit on a Google Form one week, a ticketing app the next, a WhatsApp thread the week after.

It works, in its way. The parties are real, and plenty of them are very good. What you rarely get is any sense of the room itself: who it is for, how it feels at ten o'clock, whether you could walk in alone and leave knowing four new people.

A flyer tells you who is playing. It almost never tells you what the night will be like to stand in, and that is the question most people are actually asking.

The rooms have the audiences

Look at who people follow. When we checked on 19 October, Revel had about 58,000 followers on Instagram, Yes Please about 52,000 and Clubhouse about 40,000. The Palace, the BGC complex that holds all three, had about 55,000 of its own.

The hosts and collectives who fill rooms like these on a given night mostly sit in the low thousands, and many sit well below that. The venues have built the audiences. The people who throw the nights usually have a flyer, a DM inbox and a group chat.

And between the two, almost nobody writes anything down. Some of the city's most interesting nights belong to collectives that Rolling Stone Philippines has written about, and several of them have no account you can easily find. A good night happens, the photos go up for a weekend, and then it is gone.

What Party Life covers

Party Life MNL is a nightlife magazine for Manila, and we mean the word. We cover the rooms: the rooftops above Makati and BGC, the clubs of The Palace, the late bars and collective nights of Poblacion. Each piece tells you what kind of night a place is for, so you can choose before you go.

We also host. Every night we put on is an issue, and every issue has a venue on the cover. The first is The Guest List: eighty guests, a rooftop or cocktail bar in BGC or Makati, ₱1,500 with two drinks included, 21 and over, and a host whose whole job is to introduce you to people.

The two halves feed each other. A magazine that hosts its own nights can tell you plainly what a room is like, because it has stood in it with eighty people. A host with a magazine can give a venue something that lasts past the weekend: a story with the room's name on the cover.

Interactive, in plain terms

We call Party Life interactive media. In practice, that comes down to three things.

  • Readers vote. The next cover venue is chosen by DM, so the room you want to see is a room you can ask for.
  • Guests become cover stars. The people in the room are the story of every issue, and the recap is built around them.
  • Venues become issues. Each night is one room, one cover and one name on the masthead.

Most nightlife media speaks in one direction. We would like to hear from you first, then go and host the night you asked for.

Where we start

We are launching now, so there is no back catalogue yet and we will not pretend otherwise. What we have is a point of view: Manila's rooms deserve to be covered the way a good magazine covers a city, with care, with names and with the people who were there.

Issue 01 is The Guest List. If you want in, DM "LIST" to @partylifemanila. If there is a room you think belongs on a cover, send us its name. The next issue is partly yours to decide.

Over the coming weeks we will publish field guides to the rooms we think about most: The Palace in BGC, the late streets of Poblacion, and the rooftops above the skyline. Read them, argue with them, and tell us what we missed.
