The catalogue

Cut the slots down to the ones people actually play

The first request of the call, and the one with the clearest reasoning behind it.

0:00:52Client
“I want to cut down a lot of slot games. Leave me like a couple of the popular ones — very popular ones — and leave me the live casino.”
0:02:11Client
“I heard from some of the customers that some of the slots didn't work, and some of the games didn't work too. So the more we have, I don't think it's good.”The real reason: a long catalogue is a long list of things that can be broken.
0:02:26Client
“Just something complete. Not too much, not too little. Not too much — it's enough.”
💰

Listing games is free; only play costs. Confirmed twice on the call: "No matter how many games you put on your website, the only thing you will be paid is generated by the GGR." So the cull is purely about quality and reliability, not about licence fees — which makes the client's instinct the right one and removes the usual counter-argument.

Agreed
A top-20 list to choose from

The platform side will compile the most popular titles and send them over for selection. Game types were mentioned too — crash and mini-games alongside slots — so the list should span categories, not just slots.

Platform side
Keeps
Live casino stays in full

Not part of the cull. By the end of the call it had turned over around a million, which is the strongest performance signal in the meeting.

Sportsbook

Second-half lines, even with small limits

Raised at the start and again at the end, which is usually a sign of what somebody actually cares about.

The ask
2nd-half NFL and college football

"NFL has these the most, college football we have in half, second half too."

The concession
Cap them low

He offered the limit himself — "we can limit them by the maximum limit, down to like a thousand or two thousand." Settled at two thousand.

Why it matters to him
Visibility, not volume

"I want it to show in there. We can make the limit low, but we have to show it." A missing market reads as a lesser product.

Blocked
No teasers

"We don't have teaser — they don't want to provide a teaser." A provider decision, not a platform one.

📞

Both items depend on the game operator rather than on the platform: "We will keep tracing and discuss with our game operator, and we will ask them to open the function." That is an external dependency with no date attached.

Look and feel

"Can we make our website look a little more colorful?"

Prompted by a competitor site the client pulled up on his phone and liked — crypto-facing, busy, bright.

Wanted
Live scores on the listing

Shown the way the competitor does it, so a browsing customer sees a game in progress rather than a list of names. "I want the live game that have the live score, like posting like this."

Wanted
Brighter colours

The current background is blue. "Make it look a little brighter." The design team will produce one or two options to review.

Already there
Hong Kong odds format

Asked for, and confirmed as already supported.

Constraint
The game iframe is not ours to restyle

"For the game operator they have their own design for their iframe. So if you want another client-self outlook, we need to discuss with the game provider too."

🎨

Two different surfaces are being conflated here, and it will cause a disappointment later. The site chrome — background, nav, colours, how fixtures are listed — is the platform's to change. The game window inside it belongs to the provider and comes styled as they ship it. A "brighter site" will still contain an unchanged game area, and the client should be told that before he sees the mock-ups rather than after.
