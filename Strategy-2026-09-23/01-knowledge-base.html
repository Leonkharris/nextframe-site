📁

Start in Google Drive. Not because it is the right final home, but because "everyone picks it up easily and finds it simple" — and a structure nobody maintains is worth nothing. Deeper database systems are harder to operate day to day, and the people filling it are not engineers.

The closing argument: "DB migration is fast. Building a DB is not that simple." So optimise the first step for adoption, and the second for correctness.

Why not Notion
It is a text tool, not a store

Hard to centralise, hard to search, and poor with images and video. The verdict on the call: it works as a text-based project-management tool, not as a central repository. "It's clearly difficult to draw that kind of organised, structured folder architecture in Notion."

The uncomfortable part
This was written down once already

"Do you remember, early on I made a document on file naming and management paths? You've probably forgotten — it was a long time ago." It was written partly to prepare for AI search. The convention existed and lapsed; a second one will lapse too unless somebody owns it.

B2B

The point is round-trip time. Today a partner question goes to the game provider and comes back days later.

A concrete first use, proposed on the call. Many game providers expose an API, and the team already uses Telegram groups. The reality check

A perfect database answers about a third of the questions

The most useful intervention in the meeting, and the one most likely to be forgotten. It came from someone who had actually looked at what partners ask.

📉

"Partners and clients ask about game lists and creative assets — but from what I've observed that's only about 30%. The rest includes account-level problems, and real issues the game provider is having on their own side. That is not something we can pull from our own data at all."

And on the scale of the job: "Capturing the knowledge is the core of it first — and that's only about 20% of the thing."

What the database fixes
Game lists, assets, documented policy

Roughly a third of inbound questions, and the third that currently wastes the most time.

What it does not
Account issues and provider faults

Account problems need an interface into live account data, not a document store. Provider-side faults are not ours to know. Three separate problems were being treated as one.

🪜

The staged plan that came out of it is better than the original. Deploy the bot first not to answer questions but to log them — collect partner contacts and record what people actually ask, have it summarise, and then locate where the real problems are. Build the answers against evidence instead of against assumption.
