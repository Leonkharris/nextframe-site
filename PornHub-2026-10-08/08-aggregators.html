The stalled negotiation

Peter & Sons will not come down, because we have no volume

The conversation opened with a report from a call that had just finished with the Peter & Sons sales contact.

Their position
List price 5%, minimum 5%

4.5% has been quoted to RP. The reluctance to extend it is explicitly about volume: giving the same rate to someone with no numbers devalues it.

The counter
Three months, then judge us

"I want a promotion for three months, and a low GGR. In these three months you can see my numbers, and in the fourth month you can give me what I want."

The precedent
GameArt gave 40% off

Forty per cent off for the first three months, which is exactly the structure being asked for here — enough runway to hit a target before the rate resets.

The leverage
Combine two buyers

Jin Fu Long is already negotiating with Peter & Sons separately. The suggestion: add the two volumes together and go back with one number instead of two small ones.

🤝

The contact. Peter & Sons' Asia manager — Estella in the transcript, spelling unconfirmed — is Taiwanese, Chinese-speaking, based in Ireland, and described as the only person covering the entire Chinese-speaking market for them. She indicated she could do 4.5% for social casino in Taiwan, confidentially, because another vendor is on 5% for social.

Two things follow from that. First, the relationship is personal and single-threaded, which is leverage for us and a risk for them. Second, a rate given "don't tell anybody else" is not a rate that survives being quoted in a group chat.

The bigger idea

Be the middleman for the whole Taiwan market

Out of a stalled half-point negotiation came the strategic proposal of the meeting.

Western game providersSlotmill, Massive Gaming, GameArt, Peter & Sons — no Chinese-speaking staff between them
Us, in the middleNegotiating terms and monthly promotions in both directions. "We can actually ask for more promotion month by month, so even with the list price GGR they can still get some of the promotion."
Taiwanese aggregators and operatorsRP, Jin Fu Long and the rest — united rather than bidding against each other
The pitch to vendors
We manage your Asian clients

One person covers the whole Chinese-speaking market for Peter & Sons. We offer to carry that load — negotiating and servicing on both sides.

The pitch to operators
We get you a rate you can't get alone

Speed and language. "We can go really fast with the vendors to negotiate all the terms and conditions."

The defensive logic
Stop the price war

"Everybody dropping price and then it can start to become a price war… If we can be like an association which stands in the middle, which controls all people who are selling the same price, then we're fine."

The hole in it
We can be disintermediated

Named on the call: if Jin Fu Long goes directly to Estella, "that means that there's no need for us." The position rests on being faster and better, not on owning anything.

📅

An industry change may do this for them. The vendor side is reportedly planning to impose a minimum selling percentage by region from next year — for example, nothing below 5% in Asia regardless of who is reselling. If that happens, the margin competition disappears and the service argument is all that is left, which is the position this plan is trying to build anyway. Unconfirmed, second-hand, worth tracking.

Useful side findings

Two things worth keeping out of the rest of the conversation

CP Gaming is Chinese-owned

A correction made live on the call

  • The owner is Chinese; there is a separate Chinese-speaking team
  • Previously assumed otherwise by at least one person on the call
  • They have been reluctant to touch the Asian market
  • They have now agreed to open for social casino and i-gaming after being shown our client volumes

Why a game wins in Taiwan

Nobody can predict it, including the provider

  • Even GameArt cannot explain why its biggest Taiwan title took off
  • The only common factor found: very large multipliers — 20,000x, 50,000x
  • Discovery runs through Instagram and influencers posting big wins
  • So the market is gambler-led, not content-led
📊

The reporting insight, which generalises well beyond Taiwan. From a previous report referenced on the call: the top ten games by number of players and the top ten by amount wagered are not the same ten games. Popularity and revenue come apart, and they come apart game by game, not provider by provider. The closing observation on it: operators generally know which games they want, and do not know or care which provider supplies them.

That is an argument for selling and reporting at game level rather than provider level, in pitches and in the weekly numbers.
