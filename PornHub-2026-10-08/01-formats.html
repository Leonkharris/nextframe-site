The recommendation

Start with banners, but do not rule out the aggressive formats

The team had said it wanted to begin with banners. The account manager agreed as a starting point but pushed back on treating the rest as off-limits, because the heavy formats are where the volume is.

The three sizes he named unprompted — 300x250, 305x99 (or 300x100, the same thing) and 320x480 — because all three sit at the very top of the page. "It's high visibility and the users will see your ads." Those sizes drive the whole placement plan on the next page.

The formats, one by one

What each one actually is

The gallery lives at trafficjunky.com/advertiser/ad-formats. He scrolled it live; these are the cards as they appeared. Note that the left-hand navigation highlight lags behind the scroll position, so the highlighted name often does not match the cards on screen — read the card headings, not the sidebar.

1

Header — the strongest banner

Mobile 305x99 and 300x100. Sits at the very top of the page, so it is the first thing a user sees. He rated it the strongest performer of the banner family.

Underplayer — cheaper, weaker

Same dimensions, placed directly under the video on a video page. "The CPMs are lower for underplayer compared to the header, but generally it's not that expensive… I would say the header will be a stronger contender."

Interstitial — a dedicated page

Mobile 300x250. After a few video clicks the user is taken to a blank dedicated page carrying only the ad, held there for about ten seconds, then returned to where they were.

Asked directly how it appears: "it'll be blank and then you'll just see the ad… after 10 seconds it will automatically get shifted back to the page that we're on, or they would just need to click continue." The old X close button appears to be gone.
2

Native — the one to avoid

Native blends into the video thumbnail grid: a still image with a headline underneath, looking like another video. Both Native Static and Native Rollover are 640x360 on desktop and mobile.

He was explicit that it is the wrong format for this vertical: "The natives, I don't see any performance for your style of verticals. I would avoid that… It works a lot better with dating offers or cam offers. They're very similar to whatever's on our page."

His reasoning is worth keeping: native works when the ad looks like the content. A casino ad never will, on this site.
3

In-Stream Video — the pre-roll

16:9, desktop and mobile. This is the traditional pre-roll that plays before the video. The user gets five guaranteed seconds with no skip button, after which "you can skip ad" appears. A click anywhere on the player goes to the landing page.

He rated it the format to test if the team will not run popunders: "It's a very big format, a long-form video style format, so it is engaging… for sure you guys should test, because it's a very strong ad format."

4

Out-Stream Video — new, and silent

Also 16:9, mobile. A recently released format that blends into the thumbnail grid like native, but the video actually plays as the user scrolls. The critical difference from in-stream: out-stream carries no audio at all, while in-stream can run with or without.

There was confusion on the call because the left menu lists three similar entries. He clarified: there are only two buyable options, in-stream (pre-roll) and out-stream (the blending one). "In and out stream videos" is just a category heading.

Design consequence: an out-stream creative has to work mute. No voiceover, no audio sting, everything legible on screen.
5

Shorties — vertical, Pornhub only

Mobile 480x854, a 9:16 vertical. The platform's TikTok-style format: scrolling through short videos, an ad appears after every ninth. Currently Pornhub only, with expansion to the other sites planned.

His honest assessment: "It's fairly new… the traffic-wise it's not a lot because it is a new ad format, but it is an option for you guys to test as well."

Also on this screen: Banner CTA Image, a 32x32 icon that displays during an in-stream video ad and is added while creating the pre-roll campaign. It is not a standalone buy.
