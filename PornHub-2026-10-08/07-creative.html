The mismatch

"Their website is pretty much like Asian style"

Molly raised it as an aside and it turned into the most consequential targeting idea of the call.

0:20:55Molly
“We're trying to fit kinds of US customers for them, but actually their website is pretty much like Asian style. Very Asian, I would say.”
0:21:10Molly
“If we're targeting for that US customer or player, actually I don't think they will like to play this kind of outfit or the UI.”The creative can win the click and still lose the player at the landing page.
0:21:30Molly
“I think it's better that we can target for American Chinese, or American Vietnamese.”The proposal: narrow to Asian-American audiences inside the US rather than buy the US broadly.
0:21:44Juan Carlos
“Back to their website — it does look very Asian.”Agreed, not debated.
🎯

This was agreed in principle and then dropped. No one took it to the next step, and it has real consequences the group did not work through:

TrafficJunky's step-1 targeting offers device, format, content category and gender. Nothing in the wizard, as it was demonstrated, targets ethnicity or language. If the Asian-American audience is the real target, it has to be reached through creative — language, faces, game titles, cultural cues — or through a source and geo mix, not through a targeting checkbox. Nobody assigned that work, and the twenty creatives already built are in English with Western casting.

What is already built

Twenty creatives, two very different directions

Leon shared the Midnight Circuit creative page during the call. The sets split cleanly into two pitches, which is useful — they are a real A/B test rather than twenty variations of the same idea.

1

The safety set

Six creatives built around trust and discretion rather than winning. The headlines are "Your Money. Your Privacy. Protected.", "Locked Down. Paid Out.", "Licensed. Encrypted. Discreet.", "Nothing On Your Statement But You.", "Withdraw In Minutes. Not Days." and "A Safe Place To Play."

Calls to action are PLAY SECURE, CASH OUT and PLAY FREE. The art is slot thumbnails — Fortune Tiger, Fortune Rabbit, Lucky Neko, Gates of Olympus — a gold shield with a padlock, and a phone showing a big win. Every card carries the brand flag logo and 18+ Play responsibly.

"Nothing On Your Statement But You" is the sharpest idea in the set: it is the one headline written specifically for someone browsing an adult site, and it addresses a real objection.

The creative argument

A hot girl is not a creative idea on a porn site

Juan Carlos made the one observation that should govern everything built from here.

💡

"It's not only about like a really sexy girl — you're in a porn site, there's like 2000 hot girls on the site. So it's not only about a really sexy girl, but also like the whole design as itself."

On this platform, sex appeal is the wallpaper. It cannot be the differentiator, because the surrounding page outcompetes any banner on that axis. Whatever makes someone stop has to be something the page around it is not already doing — a joke, a shock, a familiar object, a claim.

😂
Memes

Juan Carlos's main proposal. "Most people who watch porn probably get attention from the memes." Humour is the thing the surrounding page is not supplying.

🎰
Slot characters, made sexual

Take the recognisable Pragmatic and GameArt mascots players already know from the casino and push them in the direction the platform expects. Familiarity plus innuendo.

🎵
A catchy pre-roll

His reference was a Brazilian music pre-roll that ran before videos and became widely known on the strength of the song alone.

😏
Crude slogan humour

Several double-entendre taglines pairing an anatomical joke with "win big". Crude, on-platform, and cheap to produce. A real option, not a throwaway.

⚖️

The counter-argument came from Molly and it is the one that holds. On the music pre-roll idea: "If they're doing a pre-roll, it means they're actually doing the brand exposure. But right now, what concerns us is that our clients will need to see the numbers."

Brand-building creative is the right answer for a brand with runway. This campaign has roughly twenty-five days of budget at full tilt and a client checking performance daily. Whatever gets built has to be judged on click-through within a week or two, which argues for the direct, claim-led creative over the memorable one — at least for the first flight.
