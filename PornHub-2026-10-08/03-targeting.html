The US complication

Safe for work, not safe for work, and the age gate

This was the longest explanation of the call and the easiest thing to get wrong. It is worth reading twice.

1

Why the SFW version exists

Several US states now require age verification to view adult content. The network's response was a safe-for-work version of its sites that anyone can reach without verifying. To go through to the NSFW version, a user in one of those states has to submit ID.

"Because of that, our platform released a safe-for-work version where anybody can access it. And then after, if they want to access the not safe for work, they would have to submit their IDs and follow the verification process."

So in those states there is a gate, and there is an audience sitting in front of the gate that a NSFW-only campaign never reaches.
2

His instruction for a US campaign

Target both. "Because we're targeting US, I would target both of the options on our platform… especially for US it's worth targeting both of them, just so that you have the maximum exposure."

A SFW campaign puts the ad in front of users at the gate, before they have verified. A NSFW campaign reaches them after. Running only one of the two leaves a measurable share of the US audience untouched.

Content Rating is set on step 1 of the wizard, next to the campaign name. It is a per-campaign choice, so covering both means two campaigns — which doubles the daily minimum spend. Factor that into the four-ad plan.
⚠️

The trap, in his words. "Even if you're running a Not Safe for Work campaign and you're targeting US as a whole, it will still target all of US. It's just that your ads will only appear to users for particular states that have logged into the Not Safe for Work version."

So a NSFW campaign reporting low volume in a given state is not a targeting mistake and should not be "fixed" by narrowing geo. It is the age gate. "You don't necessarily need to do not safe for work and target a particular state. You just have to keep in mind that if you see a low traffic volume, the reason why is because there's not a lot of traffic for a particular state that's only not safe for work."

The source list for a 300x250 mobile banner

A draft campaign at 300x250 offers 39 sources across two pages, each with Min CPM, Suggested CPM and Your CPM. Everything is excluded by default and gets switched on individually.

The spread is wide. In this draft, Spankbang Mobile's middle banner shows a minimum of $0.050, Fanhub's footer $0.010, Pornhub Mobile SFW Interstitial $0.010, Pornhub Mobile Interstitial $0.220, and one small source sits at $2.000. Pornhub is not the cheapest inventory on the list — it is the deepest.

There is a Bulk Edit Selected CPMs control, so bids do not have to be set row by row. The table sorts by site.
Campaign structure

What actually targets, and what is just filing

A direct question about Group and Labels produced a useful distinction.

Filing only — no effect

For your own organisation

  • Group — defaults to "General"
  • Labels — free text

"These are just for your internal purposes. This has nothing to do with the campaign or targeting… it'll just show in your campaigns tab."

Real targeting

Everything else on step 1

  • Device — All / Desktop / Mobile
  • Ad Format — Display / In-Stream / Pop / Out-stream
  • Format Type — Banner / Native (/ Scrollable on mobile)
  • Ad Type — Static Banner / Video Banner
  • Ad Dimensions — one only, per campaign
  • Content Category — Straight, gay, trans, etc.
  • Gender — All, or narrowed
  • Content Rating — NSFW or SFW
🗂️

Use the labels anyway. They do nothing to delivery, but with a four-ad flight spread across two content ratings and potentially several dimensions, the campaigns tab fills up quickly. A naming convention agreed now — the existing two campaigns are already named Mobile - Popunder - PH (USA) and …- Segments — costs nothing and keeps the weekly client report readable.
