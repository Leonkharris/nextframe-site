The arithmetic

The number that decides everything else

Juan Carlos put it to the account manager during the first call and then repeated it to the team: the platform will not let an ad run on less than twenty-five US dollars a day. That single floor sets the ceiling on how many ads can be live at once.

The floor
$25 per ad, per day

Not per campaign — per ad. Nothing can be dialled below it, so the only lever is how many ads are switched on.

The multiplication
16 ads = $400 a day

The creative set prepared for this client runs to sixteen pieces. All sixteen live is four hundred dollars a day before anybody looks at a result.

The runway
Roughly 25 days

"In 20 days, we won't have any budget." Corrected on the call to 25. Either way the money has a visible end date.

🧮

What the 25 days implies about the budget. Four hundred dollars a day for twenty-five days is ten thousand dollars. Nobody stated the budget outright, but Molly later sized her own planning against "a budget like under, like around like 10k", and the TrafficJunky account seen in the first call was sitting at roughly the same figure. Treat $10,000 as the working number until someone confirms it in writing.

The counter-proposal

Four to eight ads, not sixteen

Two different arguments landed on the same answer, from opposite directions.

1

Juan Carlos: you need room left over to test

Running everything at once does not just spend faster, it removes the ability to learn anything. "Maybe four, or I don't know, up to eight ads at the same time, no more than that, because then we won't have enough for testing — this A and B testing, we don't have enough."

His second point was about duration rather than count: the flight should last one to two months, because a single ad will not perform like another and the team needs time to rotate through them — and through positions, "one pre-roll, one pop-up, one banner in front."

Sequencing, not simultaneity. Different ads at different times, different placements at different times.
2

Molly: we do not yet know what a result costs

Her objection was that every plan on the table assumed a cost per click that nobody has measured on this platform. "Right now one thing is that we also don't know what is actually per cost. Because the cost is ideal number."

Her recommendation was to start below whatever looks reasonable, find the real cost, and only then size the flight. She also warned about the direction of travel: "what if the cost goes up and we will run the budget earlier than we expect."

She described how the networks behave: "they always run with very little until they know that this content has good conversion, they add the budget."
The benchmark

What the same team spends on Meta

Molly runs the client's Meta advertising, and the comparison she gave is the only real cost data in the meeting. It is also the point where the comparison breaks down.

Meta, US market

What the team actually spends today

  • 40–60 ads running together
  • $80–100 total across all of them, first week
  • Message objective: $3–4 per message
  • Click objective: under $1 per click
  • Budget can be set per ad at almost any level

TrafficJunky

What this platform forces

  • 4–8 ads is already the stretch
  • $100–200/day minimum for that many
  • No message objective at all
  • CPM buying only — cost per click is derived, not bought
  • $25/day floor per ad, non-negotiable
⚠️

The benchmark does not transfer, and this was not resolved on the call. Molly's instinct — "it is better that we set the budget even lower" — is a Meta habit. On Meta the team spent eighty to a hundred dollars across sixty ads in a week. On TrafficJunky, four ads at the platform minimum is $700 in a week, seven times that, and there is no setting that makes it cheaper. Juan Carlos said the quiet part out loud: "I don't think 25 dollars will make any difference."

Someone needs to confirm with the account manager whether the $25 floor is a hard platform rule or an account-level setting that can be lowered for a test. Nothing in either recording answers that.

The client

More budget is possible, but only against numbers

The question of whether the budget could simply be increased came up twice, and the answer both times was conditional.

The opening
The client will top up

Relayed on the call: as long as there is good traction on clicks and actions, the client is open to adding budget.

The condition
They check the numbers daily

Molly's caution. The client reviews performance every day, and a weekly report goes to them. Good numbers buy more budget; weak numbers freeze it.

The catch
Clicks are not what they judge

"The clients not only care about the click, they will pretty much care about how many people will do the promotion or for the following up." Second deposits appear in the report too.

The problem
None of that is measurable here

No pixel is being installed, so registrations, promotions claimed and second deposits cannot be attributed to a Pornhub ad. See the back end.

🚨

This is the central unresolved risk of both meetings. The client releases more budget on the strength of registrations and deposits. The platform can report impressions, clicks and click-through rate, and will show conversions as zero. Nobody on either call proposed how to close that gap.

The gap may already be closed and nobody noticed: twenty channel-coded tracking links were built for this campaign and were on screen during the first call. The back-end page sets out what still needs confirming for them to count as measurement.

What is missing

Inferred as $10,000 from two indirect references. Never stated, never written down, never confirmed with the client in the recording.
