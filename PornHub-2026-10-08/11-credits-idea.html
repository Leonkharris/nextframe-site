The idea

"They want porn credits instead of really like play"

It came out of a complaint about generic creative, and the reasoning is sharper than the delivery.

0:08:24—
“If it's just ordinary content — for example just saying 'this is WinFun777' — commonplace, nobody really cares, you know.”The problem being solved.
0:08:53—
“Maybe, for example, get free Pornhub credits through the games… this is more eye-catching, because people who want to watch porn, they want porn credits instead of really like play.”
0:10:00—
“If I want to watch porn and I want a porn credit, if I want to get free porn credits, that means that I have to go through this website.”The mechanism: the credit is the reason to cross from one site to the other.
0:12:04—
“A player goes through that link because they want Pornhub or RedTube, YouPorn or Tube8 credits — they can go there and claim the promotion from WinFun777… and they can top up, for example, $10, $5, to their Pornhub account.”The full shape of it.
💡

Why this is the strongest idea in either October meeting. Every other creative approach fights for attention against a page that is better at holding it. This one does not compete — it offers the one thing the audience is already there for, and makes the casino the route to getting it. It also solves the measurement problem by construction: a credit can only be claimed through a tracked link and a registration.

The blocker

Nobody knows whether the network will do it

1

The answer they have is from a chatbot

"I checked on [an AI chatbot]. It says currently I don't think we can top up the credits on the player's accounts." That is the entire basis for believing it cannot be done.

A general-purpose model has no visibility into what an ad network will agree to commercially. This is not an answer, it is a guess with a confident tone.
2

The person who can actually answer is named

The action was assigned twice in the same breath: "It would be better if you can ask Franco… please ask Franco, would it be possible if we can top up the credits."

Franco is the network-side contact. He is the only route to a real yes or no, and as of the 8 October meeting three days later, the question had not been raised with the account manager either.

This is the single cheapest open item across all four October meetings. It is one message, and the answer changes the whole creative approach.
The idea it replaced

A porn-star endorsement, correctly rejected

The group had previously asked about a collaboration with a performer. The answer came back, and then the reasoning against it came back harder.

What was possible
Direct contact with a performer

Relayed from the network side: it would be a direct arrangement with the actor or actress. One specific performer was mentioned as available.

Why it was dropped
An endorsement buys nothing

"What are you going to do with that celebrity? They're not going to… unless they can claim a credit to go through their OnlyFans, that is worth promoting. But if it's just asking a celebrity to be a representative, I don't think that's the catch."

🌍

A second objection was geographic: the campaign targets the United States, and the performer available was not a fit for that market. Which runs straight into the audience problem on the next page — the US may be the wrong target for this client in the first place.
