The concept

You walk in off a New York street — and suddenly, you're in Tokyo.

Not the polished version. The real one. Layered. Electric. Alive.

Tokyo Rocks is a modern Japanese food hall and immersive entertainment destination inspired by the energy, nightlife, food culture, music and sensory experiences of Tokyo — a multi-dimensional cultural destination, not a single restaurant.

Inside Tokyo Rocks

One destination.
Endless experiences.

01 · Ground floor

The food hall and market

A market-style Japanese food hall built for movement and discovery — open kitchens, counters and stalls around a central floor, with a full retail grocery and sake wall running alongside it.

CountersSushi & seafood, ramen & noodles, robata skewers, katsu sando
RetailJapanese groceries, sake & shochu, artisanal goods, coffee & tea
FormatFast-casual through to fine dining in one room
TradingAll day, 11am to 11pm, plus late-night

"Eataly meets Tokyo nightlife."

02 · Retail

The grocery and sake market

A Tsukiji-inspired market hall inside the venue — produce, pantry, prepared food and a two-storey sake and shochu wall. Retail that trades all day, carries its own margin, and gives guests a reason to walk in when they are not eating.

SectionsFish market, produce, bento & deli, sake & shochu
AlsoArtisanal goods, home & design, coffee & tea
03 · Second floor

Fire and craft

The second floor is the room people come back for. An open robatayaki grill is the visual and social heart of the venue; hidden alongside it, an intimate omakase counter delivers the quiet, high-check counterpoint.

RobatayakiOpen flame — wagyu, black cod, king salmon, scallop, Japanese vegetables
OmakaseIntimate counter, seasonal, chef-led
Also on twoSake Library and private dining room
PairingSake, Japanese whisky and cocktails

炉端焼き LIVE FIRE. EVERY DISH. EVERY MOMENT.

04 · Reservation

The omakase counter

Quiet, refined and precise — an intimate counter hidden inside the energy of the hall. The highest check average in the building and the room that sets the culinary credibility of everything around it.

PrinciplePrecision. Seasonality. Experience.
FormatChef-led seatings at a fixed counter
05 · Evening

Whisky and vinyl

A listening lounge of rare whisky, artisanal sake and curated vinyl. Low light, low cost of goods, high margin — the room that carries the evening between dinner service and the late-night bar.

Cellar300+ whiskies, artisanal sake, rare finds
SoundCurated vinyl, listening sessions
06 · Late night

The rock & roll bar

By night another door opens. A hidden underground music bar inspired by the rock clubs of Shibuya and Shinjuku — live bands, vinyl DJs, Japanese whisky and late-night dining that takes the venue through to close.

ProgrammingLive bands, DJ sets, ticketed shows, artist activations
TradeBeverage-led, late-night food

Live loud 大きな音で生きる

07 · Ticketed

Immersive Tokyo

A 360° projection environment with interactive floors, sound and light that recreates the feeling of walking the streets of Tokyo. Admissions revenue that trades independently of the kitchen — and converts into food, drink and retail on the way out.

Technology360° video walls, projection mapping, interactive floors
RevenueAdmissions, private hire, brand activations
08 · Ticketed

Culture and costume

A kimono and geisha costume gallery with a dressing and photography experience, alongside a pop-culture museum of Japanese animation art, exhibits and collectibles. The daytime and family draw, and the most photographed rooms in the building.

GalleryKimono from the Edo period to modern, expert styling, photography
MuseumAnime art, exhibits, collectibles and retail

Any use of third-party anime or character IP requires a separate licence; the museum programme should be underwritten on original and licensed-in content.

09 · The signature

The Sumo Hall

A dramatic ring at the centre of the second floor with live exhibition matches, taiko drums, ceremonial entrances and sake service — the room that makes Tokyo Rocks impossible to compare to another restaurant, and the single most defensible reason to buy a ticket.

The structure

Operations and global IP are held separately

NEWCO

Delaware operating company

  • Holds the New York lease and operating assets
  • Raises $18M for 30% investor ownership
  • Operates the venue and distributes cash flow
BRANDCO

Global intellectual-property company

  • Owned by Todd English, CEO & Founder
  • Owns Tokyo Rocks trademarks, brand and IP
  • Licenses defined rights to NEWCO

Investor diligence must define the license term, royalty, exclusivity, termination rights and change-of-control treatment.

Uses

Required closing deliverable: line-item development budget, monthly cash flow, contingency policy and committed funding sources.

Debt

Debt can accelerate returns — but must not endanger the flagship

Recommended credit guardrails
  • No uncommitted funding gap at construction start
  • Debt sized to conservative cash flow — not headline revenue
  • Board approval for new debt, liens and guarantees
  • Interest reserve and minimum liquidity covenant
  • No cross-collateralization of global BRANDCO IP
Investor diligence

Debt schedule must show

  • Lender, principal, rate and maturity
  • Amortization and balloon exposure
  • Covenants, guarantees and collateral
  • Base / downside debt-service coverage

Current deck does not disclose a debt amount or lender commitment.

The model

It must prove resilience — not just upside

Base case

Opening date, stabilized sales, margins and cash yield

Downside

Construction delay, lower traffic, margin pressure and slower ramp

Break-even

Monthly revenue required to cover fixed costs and debt service
